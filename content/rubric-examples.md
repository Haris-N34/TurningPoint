# Evidence rubric examples

These are reviewer calibration examples, not model answers candidates must copy. Strong names a choice, reason, trade-off and effect on people. Some gives a reason but leaves the trade-off or consequence unclear. Not observed means this answer supplies no evidence for that skill; it does not mean the person lacks it. Missing or unusable transcripts need human review instead.

Review the candidate's words and decisions only. Never infer capability from face, emotion, voice tone, accent, grammar, fluency, university or identity. Equivalent reasoning in different dialects deserves equivalent treatment. There is no overall score or automatic rejection. Quote evidence verbatim and link to its checkpoint; discard an unmatched quote.

## problem-solving

**Strong:** “I broke the delivery problem into storage and transport because the food needed cooling first. I borrowed a trolley instead of hiring a van, but asked vendors to make two trips.”

**Some:** “I fixed the delivery problem because the food needed to arrive before opening.”

**Not observed:** “The hall opens on Saturday.” This describes the setting without explaining a problem or response.

## adaptability

**Strong:** “I changed to a smaller market because the grant fell through. Instead of keeping every stall, I kept food access and told vendors they would share tables.”

**Some:** “I changed the layout because we had less money.”

**Not observed:** “I followed the original plan.” There is no evidence of a response to changed conditions.

## resilience

**Strong:** “We tried again after the venue cancelled because residents still needed the service. We finished in a smaller hall, but volunteers gave up an afternoon to move equipment.”

**Some:** “I kept going because the community needed the market.”

**Not observed:** “Everything went as expected.” No setback or recovery is described.

## ambiguity

**Strong:** “I accepted the risk of ordering fewer meals because attendance was uncertain. Instead of guessing high, I arranged a reserve with vendors so late arrivals still had an option.”

**Some:** “I made the call without knowing attendance because the order deadline was today.”

**Not observed:** “The confirmed order was fifty meals.” No decision under uncertainty is explained.

## prioritization

**Strong:** “I protected cold storage first because food safety mattered most. I cut decorations instead, but explained to volunteers why their display work would wait.”

**Some:** “I put cold storage first because food needed to stay safe.”

**Not observed:** “There were tables, food and decorations.” The answer lists tasks without choosing between them.

## creativity

**Strong:** “I borrowed folding tables because our rental budget was gone. Instead of cancelling stalls, we reused classroom furniture, but asked the team to allow extra setup time.”

**Some:** “I borrowed tables because renting them was too expensive.”

**Not observed:** “We had ten tables.” No resourceful action is described.

## collaboration

**Strong:** “I split the work with the team because one coordinator could not cover every stall. Instead of assigning shifts alone, I asked volunteers to swap, but kept a named backup for each role.”

**Some:** “I asked the team to share tasks because setup was running late.”

**Not observed:** “Five volunteers arrived.” Presence alone does not show shared work or listening.

## empathy

**Strong:** “I listened to a vendor who was worried about losing income because the smaller layout affected her most. Instead of moving her stall without discussion, I offered shared frontage, but explained the space it cost others.”

**Some:** “I listened because the vendor was worried about sales.”

**Not observed:** “The vendor had a corner stall.” No awareness of another person's needs is shown.

## communication

**Strong:** “I told vendors about the smaller grant before posting publicly because they needed to plan stock. Instead of promising full support, I explained the cuts, but gave them a contact for questions.”

**Some:** “I emailed vendors because the opening time changed.”

**Not observed:** “The opening time was noon.” No explanation of how information reached affected people is given.

## feedback

**Strong:** “I changed the briefing because volunteers said the first instructions were unclear. Instead of defending my slides, I tested a one-page checklist with them, but delayed the start to check understanding.”

**Some:** “I changed the briefing because the team gave me feedback about confusing instructions.”

**Not observed:** “The briefing was twenty minutes.” The answer does not describe receiving or acting on feedback.

## Editorial question review

Launch Week has five eligible questions at n3, n6, n9, n10 and n12. Client Rescue in PR #36 has five eligible questions. Each asks about the actual decision's trade-off, risk or affected people without prescribing a winning choice. All are under 30 words. These fallbacks deliberately avoid fabricating a quoted choice when context is unavailable.

The local demo generates questions synchronously from the choice log; it has no network request to time out. Its missing-context path uses the node fallback immediately. A future remote implementation must use the same fallback after four seconds or on failure. This content change does not implement a network timer.

Manual review protocol: for each eligible node, try every option with several seeds, check the generated question quotes the selected label verbatim, stays within 30 words and remains neutral. Remove choice context and confirm the node fallback appears. For real-life follow-ups, ensure any quoted detail occurs in the answer. Escalate invented evidence or identity-based judgments on Board #1.

## analytical

**Strong:** “I compared the stockout data across the three stores because one chart looked like noise. The trend only held in the small stores, so instead of recommending it everywhere I told the CFO it would affect two sites first.”

**Some:** “I looked at the data because the numbers seemed off.”

**Not observed:** “The meeting was on Thursday.” No reading of data or evidence is described.

## curiosity

**Strong:** “I asked around at the café because the report didn't explain the old system failing. I found out the staff had a workaround, so instead of replacing everything we kept it, and the store team felt heard.”

**Some:** “I looked into the old report because I wanted to know why it failed.”

**Not observed:** “I finished the slides on time.” No searching for information beyond what was given is described.
